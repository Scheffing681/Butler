## The twin verses

All that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts, it is made of our thoughts. If a man speaks or acts with an impure thought, suffering follows him as the wheel follows the foot of the ox that draws the cart. (1)

All that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts, it is made of our thoughts. If a man speaks or acts with a pure thought, happiness follows him like a shadow that never leaves him. (2)

"He abused me, he beat me, he defeated me, he robbed me." In those who harbour such thoughts, hatred will never cease. (3)

"He abused me, he beat me, he defeated me, he robbed me." In those who do not harbour such thoughts, hatred will cease. (4)

For hatred never ceases by hatred in this world; hatred ceases by love. This is an eternal law. (5)

The others do not understand that we must all come to an end here. But for those who understand it, quarrels cease. (6)

As rain gets into a badly thatched house, so passion gets into an untrained mind. As rain does not get into a well-thatched house, so passion does not get into a well-trained mind. (13–14)

## On watchfulness

Watchfulness is the path to the deathless, heedlessness the path to death. The watchful do not die; the heedless are as if already dead. (21)

By effort, watchfulness, restraint and self-control, let the wise man make for himself an island which no flood can overwhelm. (25)

## On the mind

The trembling, unsteady mind, hard to guard and hard to hold back, the wise man straightens as an archer straightens his arrow. (33)

Hard to restrain, light, rushing wherever it pleases: it is good to tame such a mind. A tamed mind brings happiness. (35)

Whatever an enemy may do to an enemy, or a hater to a hater, a wrongly directed mind will do us greater harm. (42)

Neither mother nor father nor any other relative will do a man so much good as a well-directed mind. (43)

## On flowers

As the bee takes the nectar and flies away without harming the flower, its colour or its scent, so let the wise man move through the village. (49)

Do not look at the faults of others, at what others have done or left undone; look only at what you yourself have done or left undone. (50)

Like a beautiful flower, full of colour but without scent, are the fine but fruitless words of one who does not act on them. (51)

## On the fool

Long is the night to one who is awake, long is the road to one who is tired, long is the round of births to fools who do not know the true law. (60)

"These sons belong to me, this wealth belongs to me": so the fool torments himself. He does not even belong to himself; how much less sons and wealth? (62)

The fool who knows his foolishness is wise at least to that extent. But a fool who thinks himself wise is called a fool indeed. (63)

## On the wise

If you see a wise man who points out faults and rebukes, follow him as you would one who shows you hidden treasure. It will be better, not worse, for one who follows such a man. (76)

As a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, so the wise are not moved by blame or praise. (81)

As a deep lake is clear and still, so the wise become calm when they have heard the teaching. (82)

## On thousands

Better than a thousand words made of senseless sounds is one word of sense, on hearing which a man becomes calm. (100)

If one man conquers in battle a thousand times a thousand men, and another conquers himself alone, he is the greatest of conquerors. (103)

Better is one day in the life of one who sees how things arise and pass away than a hundred years in the life of one who does not see it. (113)

## On evil

Hasten towards the good and keep your mind from evil. If a man is slow in doing good, his mind delights in evil. (116)

Do not think lightly of evil, saying, "It will not come to me." Drop by drop the water-pot is filled; so the fool fills himself with evil, gathering it little by little. (121)

Do not think lightly of good, saying, "It will not come to me." Drop by drop the water-pot is filled; so the wise man fills himself with good, gathering it little by little. (122)

## On violence

All tremble at punishment, all fear death. Putting yourself in the place of another, do not kill and do not cause to kill. (129)

All tremble at punishment, life is dear to all. Putting yourself in the place of another, do not kill and do not cause to kill. (130)

Do not speak harshly to anyone; those who are spoken to will answer you in the same way. Angry speech is painful, and blows for blows will touch you. (133)

## On the self

The self is the master of the self: who else could be the master? With the self well subdued, a man finds a master such as few can find. (160)

By oneself evil is done, by oneself one is defiled; by oneself evil is left undone, by oneself one is purified. Purity and impurity belong to oneself: no one can purify another. (165)

Let no one neglect his own good for the sake of another's, however great. Having clearly seen his own good, let him be devoted to it. (166)

## On happiness

How happily we live, free from hatred among those who hate! Among people full of hatred, we live without hatred. (197)

Victory breeds hatred, for the defeated lives in sorrow. The peaceful man lives happily, having given up both victory and defeat. (201)

Health is the greatest gain, contentment the greatest wealth, trust the best of relatives, peace the highest happiness. (204)

## On anger

Let a man give up anger, let him cast away pride, let him overcome all bonds. No suffering befalls one who does not cling to name and form and who calls nothing his own. (221)

He who holds back rising anger like a rolling chariot, him I call a real driver; others only hold the reins. (222)

Overcome anger by not being angry, overcome evil by good, overcome the miser by giving, overcome the liar by truth. (223)

Speak the truth, do not yield to anger, give when asked, even if only a little. By these three steps one comes near the gods. (224)

This is an old saying, not one of today: they blame the one who sits silent, they blame the one who speaks much, they blame the one who speaks in moderation. There is no one in the world who is not blamed. (227)

## On the path

You yourself must make the effort; the Awakened Ones only show the way. (276)

"All compounded things are impermanent": when one sees this with wisdom, one turns away from suffering. This is the path to purity. (277)

From meditation springs wisdom; without meditation wisdom is lost. Knowing this double path of gain and loss, let a man conduct himself so that wisdom may grow. (282)
